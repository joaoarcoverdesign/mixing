Goal Coach

One plan a day, in your inbox

I built Goal Coach for myself, at a time when I needed focus, and it helped me start each day knowing what to do first. Now I want it to help other people the same way: one plan a day, with concrete actions and a message that speaks to you, every morning at 8 a.m. by email. I conceived, designed, built and launched the product, from the problem to the checkout.

The Problem

It wasn't a lack of options.It was a lack of focus.

During a career transition, I had too many fronts at once: looking for a job, opening an agency, building a product, using AI. When everything is a priority, nothing moves. And underneath it came the doubt: after 20 years in the industry, was I still good enough?

To-do lists and productivity apps organize what to do, but they don't remind you why you're doing it or tell you where to start today. I know I'm not the only one in that place. That's what turned a personal tool into a product.

The Experiment

Validate the needbefore writing code

I asked an AI to write messages based on my real goals and scheduled them by hand in Gmail, one each morning, for a week. The test cost nothing and answered the question that mattered.

Design Decisions

Every detailserving a daily habit

Email instead of an app

No install, no notifications to enable. The product lives where people already are every morning, and the first experience happens in under a minute.

A dark email that reads in any client

A real test on an iPhone showed the text almost invisible in dark mode. I rebuilt the template with tables, explicit backgrounds and colors and AA contrast, so no client inverts the colors.

A subscribe call that grows with the trial

On days 1 to 4 of the free trial, a discreet line with the days left. On days 5 to 7, a gold button. Value first, the sale after.

A trial ending that shows what you lose

The day after the trial ends, the daily plan arrives with the first action readable and the rest fading away. Since gradients don't work in email, the effect uses solid colors line by line.

One click to payment

In the first test, the subscribe button led to the form on the home page. Now it opens checkout with the chosen plan and the email already filled in, without exposing data in the URL.

Bilingual from the ground up

A PT/EN switcher on the site and the language saved at sign-up, used in the generated plan, the email and the checkout.

Business Model

Each generated plan costs a few cents of AI, and a full 7-day trial costs less than R$ 0.30 per person.

Trust Before Launch

Product design is alsowhat you don't see

Before going public, I reviewed the product end to end with AI as a technical partner. We found and fixed flaws a user would never see, but that would break their trust.

Protected data

I closed open database rules that allowed users' emails to be read with a public key.

Honest measurement

In-house click and open tracking, with signed events, to know what works.

Predictable sends

Each special email goes out only once, and is only marked as sent after confirmation.

Learnings

What I'd taketo the next product

Test the absence, not the interest.

"I liked it" is weak. "I missed it when it stopped" is the signal that justifies building.

The end of the trial is a product screen.

Without a clear moment to subscribe, the product simply stops arriving, and the person doesn't even notice.

Test on someone else's device.

The email that was perfect on my computer was unreadable on a user's iPhone.

Measure before you promote.

Without analytics on the site, you can't tell a lack of visits from a lack of conversion.
